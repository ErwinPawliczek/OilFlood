# Response protocols: from model to action (T22)

How U.S. responders actually turn a spill trajectory forecast into decisions, with the numbers and the sources.
Machine-readable twin: [`data/response/protocols.json`](../data/response/protocols.json) (49 rules, each with source IDs
`S1`..`S27`, locator and status; 7 pipeline steps; 7 real incidents; 16 gaps vs `backend/app/response.py`).

> **Not operational guidance.** The Federal On-Scene Coordinator (FOSC) and the Unified Command decide every action, using
> the Area Contingency Plan (ACP) and the Regional Response Team (RRT). Every figure below was read in a document opened
> while writing this file; the one exception (the 12-24 h operational period) is flagged "unverified".

## 1. The chain from forecast to action

```
spill reported -> FOSC (USCG COTP) + Unified Command -> asks NOAA Scientific Support Coordinator (SSC)
  T+0-1 h  SSC: initial trajectory (GNOME best estimate + minimum-regret envelope), oil fate (ADIOS), weather, tides/currents,
           initial resource-protection priorities                                              [S9, S7]
  T+1-4 h  trajectory map, resources-at-risk, overflights; match ETA to ACP/GRP priority sites (ICS 232/232a)  [S9, S19]
  tactic screen (numeric go/no-go gates, section 2): source control, dispersant, in-situ burn, mechanical, shoreline  [S1-S6]
  compare oil ETA with equipment lead time (USCG Tier 1: 6 h port / 12 h elsewhere; marsh booming: days)             [S16, S17]
  every operational period: ICS 202 objectives (weather/tide/current) -> ICS 204 assignments -> brief -> execute -> evaluate
  forecast re-run >= daily and recalibrated to overflights; DWH: 24/48/72 h forecasts, 2x/day early                   [S8, S9, S12]
  monitor: SMART (dispersant Tier I-III; burn PM10), overflights, SCAT; feed observations back into the model          [S1, S3, S20]
```

**Who does what.** FOSC (Coast Guard in the coastal zone: Sector Corpus Christi, Houston-Galveston, MSU Port Arthur, MSU
Houma, Sector New Orleans) decides; NOAA SSC is "the chief scientific advisor for the Coast Guard FOSC" [S5]. Dispersant
pre-approval belongs to the FOSC alone, RRT notified afterwards, results to RRT within 24 h [S1]. Burning agents: FOSC decides
but must first consult RRT-6 (EPA at minimum) with the ISB checklist, smoke-plume model, oil-fate trajectory and weather [S3, S4].

**What NOAA hands over.** Not model output alone: "a full trajectory analysis ... a narrative explanation of the forecast"
presented by the SSC, to the ASTM standard of forward trajectory plus uncertainty [S27, S7].
GNOME gives a *best estimate* and a *minimum regret* solution (areas that could be hit if wind/currents differ) [S7]. NOAA's own
skill statement: a 48-h trajectory is within 1-2 miles *if* winds, currents and windage are right; forecast winds/currents
usually are not good enough for < 1 mile after 48 h, so the model is rerun at least daily and tuned to overflights [S8].
TAP (thousands of GNOME runs) is for planning only, not for a live spill [S11, S8].

**Three windows** (NOAA/API *Characteristics of Response Strategies*, S6): (1) very early, 1-2 days, fresh oil near the
source: source control, containment, removal; (2) early, days-weeks, spread oil threatens resources: keep oil off resources
at risk, protect the most vulnerable habitat (site-specific, in ACPs); (3) later, days-months, oil stranded: habitat-appropriate
cleanup, sometimes leave to degrade. In the first 24-48 h most oils become hard to recover, burn or disperse; 1,000+ bbl can
cover 1-10 sq mi (3-26 km2) in a day or two. If on-water systems cannot handle the oil, effort may be forgone on the water to
protect and clean shoreline.

## 2. Tactic gates with numbers

| Tactic | Zone / authority | Weather and oil gates | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dispersant** (RRT-6, TX+LA) | Pre-approved: water **>= 10 m deep AND >= 3 nm** from shore ("whichever is farthest") out to 200 nm; test a 10 nm (18.5 km) circle around the source. Nearer shore: RRT concurrence case by case. Spray contractor must be under contract *before* the spill. | Aerial: wind **<= 25 kt**, visibility **>= 3 nm**, ceiling **>= 1,000 ft**, **daylight only**; boat: sea state OK for safe spraying. Oil: API < 17 difficult/impossible; 17-34.5 easily dispersed if treated promptly; 34.5-45 easily dispersed; > 45 no need. Needs "a few knots of wind and a light chop"; natural dispersion takes over near 25 kt / 10 ft waves; best on slicks a few thousandths to hundredths of an inch; dose 1-5 % of oil volume. SMART Tier I (visual) mandatory; positive feedback to continue; birds 1,000 ft clear. | S1, S5, S6, S20 |
| **In-situ burn** (RRT-6) | Pre-approved **seaward of 3 nm** off TX/LA, minus reef/bank/shoal zones and a Grand Isle zone (7 mi from town centre in onshore wind); inshore needs RRT approval; RRT-6 consulted before burning agents. | Thickness **>= 2-3 mm** (10 mm if heavy/emulsified; 1994 plan: >= 0.1 in, prefer 0.5 in); water **<= 25 %** (<= 50 % with test burn); volume **> 500 bbl per burn**; wind **<= 25 mph (21.7 kt)**, preferred **< 20 mph (17.4 kt)**; waves **<= 3 ft**; current across boom **<= 0.75 kt** (tow 0.5-0.75 kt); visibility **>= 500 ft** vertical, **>= 0.5 mi** horizontal; plume not toward population within 3 mi; sun **> 15 deg**, not at night/dawn/dusk. PM10 level of concern 150 ug/m3 (1 h). Burn efficiency 90-98 %; burn rate 0.07 gal/min/ft2. | S2, S3, S4, S6 |
| **Boom** (containment / deflection / exclusion) | ACP/GRP site strategies; USCG Tier 1 on scene in 6 h (higher-volume ports) or 12 h; mobilised within 2 h of notification. | Entrainment starts at **0.7 kt perpendicular** current (0.75 in RRT-6; > 1 kt "very high rate" of loss); draft > 1/4 water depth loses oil in shallow fast water; effectiveness falls above **2-3 ft** short-period waves; ocean-rated boom/skimmers to 6 ft Hs. Galveston Bay marsh protection needs **3-5 days** to install. | S6, S3, S15, S16, S17 |
| **Skimming** | Needs oil concentrated by boom. | Encounter rate (bbl/h) = 53.33 x swath(ft) x speed(ft/s) x thickness(in); USCG credit = nameplate x 24 h x **20 %**; waves, current, debris, viscous oil cut efficiency. Gasoline: not skimmed/boomed unless public health at risk (foam, extreme safety). | S2, S6, S15 |
| **Shoreline** | ACP/GRP priority protection areas; ESI ranks 1-10; SCAT teams. | Habitat-specific cleanup; marsh: avoid foot traffic/cutting; cosmetic cleanup avoided if more damaging than the oil. | S6, S10, S17, S19 |

Deepwater Horizon operating limits were tighter than the pre-approval in places and looser in one: ceiling **1,500 ft**,
visibility **4 nm**, no spraying within **5 nm of the source, 2 nm of any vessel, 3 nm of shore**, or in water < **33 ft**;
no operations within 3 nm of visible marine life; aircraft wind limit raised to **< 35 kt** only if the pilot could correct
drift [S12]. ISB there ran in a daily burn area **3-8 mi from the source**, never within 3 mi of source-control vessels [S3].

## 3. OilFlood rules (model output -> action)

Implementable with what the pipeline already has (tracks, oil budget, hourly wind/current at the slick, ESI cells, ports) plus
bathymetry, waves and per-step emulsion fraction. Each rule's IDs point into `protocols.json`.

| # | Rule | Rule IDs |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Brief like NOAA**: present best-guess + an uncertainty envelope (ensemble with windage 1-4 %, wind direction/speed noise <= 60 deg angle scale, current noise); give ETA as best-guess *and* earliest plausible. Say "areas of probability, not oil extent". Re-run every forecast cycle. | FCST-2..4, FCST-6 |
| R2 | **Window**: hours 0-48 = windows 1/2 (on-water + protection); after stranding = window 3 (shoreline cleanup). Flag "most oils hard to recover/burn/disperse after 24-48 h". | WIN-1, WIN-2 |
| R3 | **Dispersant OK** at time t iff: slick centroid >= 3 nm offshore AND depth >= 10 m AND (spill in TX/LA offshore zone else "needs RRT concurrence"); daylight; wind <= 25 kt (35 kt only with drift correction); visibility >= 3 nm and ceiling >= 1,000 ft when forecast available; oil API band 17-45 (34.5-45 non-persistent) and not API < 17 and water not below pour point; not emulsified beyond ~25 %; always "SMART Tier I, spray contractor under contract, FOSC approves, notify RRT". | DISP-1..8 |
| R4 | **Burn OK** iff: >= 3 nm offshore and outside exclusion/Grand Isle buffer; water fraction <= 0.25 (<= 0.5 test burn); boom-concentrated thickness >= 2-3 mm and >= 500 bbl per burn; wind <= 21.7 kt (prefer <= 17.4); waves <= 3 ft; current across boom <= 0.75 kt; sun elevation > 15 deg (LA waters 08:00-17:00 local); no population centre within 3 mi downwind; "RRT-6 consultation + SMART PM10 monitoring (150 ug/m3, 1 h)". | ISB-1..7 |
| R5 | **Boom site test**: at each ESI/GRP site compare local current (inlets/passes, not the slick centroid) with 0.7 kt: <= 0.7 containment/exclusion OK; 0.7-1 kt deflection boom at an angle to a collection point; > 1 kt "boom will not hold, use shoreline crews + skimmers downstream". Waves > 2-3 ft: boom ineffective. Draft > 1/4 depth in shallows: entrainment. | MECH-1, MECH-2, ISB-6, MECH-8 |
| R6 | **Lead time**: boom ready = 0.5 h notify + 2 h mobilise + travel from the nearest staging base (5 kt water, 35 mph road); floor 6 h in higher-volume port areas (Houston/Galveston, New Orleans, ...) and 12 h elsewhere; marsh-wide protective booming = days. If ETA < lead time: shift to shoreline cleanup and the next site (CRS "time until shoreline impact"). | MECH-6, MECH-7, WIN-3 |
| R7 | **Skimmer expectation**: encounter rate from the formula with the oil's thickness, x 20 % derating, minus storage limit; headline that recovery is a fraction (DWH skimmed 3 % / burned 5 % / dispersed 8 %; Texas City Y 18 % liquid recovery, 57 % shoreline waste). | MECH-3..5, MON-2 |
| R8 | **Shoreline priority** = ACP/GRP priority protection areas first (when loaded), then ESI 10 > 9 > 8 > 7 > rest, then ETA; add seasonal/human-use flags; "ESI only, defer to ACP/GRP" otherwise. | ESI-1..3 |
| R9 | **Closures**: USCG safety zone over the slick for responder safety; ship channel restriction near channel entrances (precedent Texas City Y, wakes push oil ashore); fisheries closures with state agencies/NOAA (DWH peak 88,522 sq mi). | MECH-8, S12 |
| R10 | **ICS output**: emit the outline as ICS-style products: objectives with weather/tide/current for the next operational period (ICS 202; the usual 12-24 h length is unverified, see ICS-2), assignments with times (ICS 204), resources-at-risk list keyed to site IDs (ICS 232). | ICS-1, ICS-2 |

## 4. What responders did vs what models predicted

| Incident | Responders did | Model / forecast vs reality | Links |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Deepwater Horizon 2010** | 24/48/72 h NOAA forecasts (2x/day to 19 May, daily after) + daily shoreline-oiling outlook; first aerial dispersant approved ~13:00 22 Apr; 411 burns (265,450 bbl, ~5 %, 3-8 mi from the source); peak 3.8 M ft boom; > 2,000 pilings defeated by pass currents; 1.07 M gal surface + 0.77 M gal subsea dispersant; peak 47,849 responders, > 6,000 vessels | Forecasts were probability areas; release rate and weathering uncertain, so shoreline volumes could not be forecast; a 45-day outlook was dropped for disagreeing with observations. OilFlood backtest: persistence beats OpenOil and ours (72 h median centroid error 39 vs 42-46 km). NOAA budget: 17 % wellhead, 5 % burned, 3 % skimmed, 8 % chem. dispersed. | [OSC report](https://library.oarcloud.noaa.gov/noaa_documents.lib/NOAA_related_docs/oil_spills/on-scene_DWH_Report_Sep2011.pdf), [RRT-6 ISB policy, App. G](https://www.glo.texas.gov/sites/default/files/resources/glo/ost/spill-response-resources/rrtvi/RRT6_ISB_Policy_Offshore_Appendix13.pdf) |
| **Texas City Y 2014** (168,000 gal IFO 380) | > 600 ft boom around the barge within hours, "minimal success" (current dragged oil through); first ORO < 2 h, 3 OROs + 2 contractors by 8.5 h; > 3,000 ft boom before dark; COTP closed the Houston Ship Channel (5 days per Texas AG; 22-25 Mar, fully open 27 Mar per IncidentNews); boom and recovery pushed into the Gulf and along Galveston Island; no dispersant; peak > 2,200 people / 18 agencies; 13 mi heavy + 40 mi light-moderate oiling at the 10 Apr peak; Matagorda Island ~120 mi SW oiled (~25 mi Gulf side); 18 % liquid recovery, 25 % evaporated/dispersed, 57 % shoreline waste; ~5 weeks | NOAA trajectory summary: NE wind carries the oil out of the Bay, onshore wind mid-week could bring tarballs back along ocean beaches. Actual: oil left the bay, moved SW, reached Matagorda ~5 days later. OilFlood: HYCOM treats Galveston Bay as land, so models strand 100 % in the bay; seeded at the bay entrance OpenOil was 15 km / 8 km from the 24 / 25 Mar overflight positions. NTSB: oil "could not be effectively contained near the accident scene"; deflection + mechanical/manual removal where stranded were the sound strategies. | [NTSB MAR-15/01](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/AccidentReports/Reports/MAR1501.pdf), [NOAA Kirby page](https://response.restoration.noaa.gov/oil-and-chemical-spills/oil-spills/kirby-barge-oil-spill-houstontexas-city-ship-channel-port-bolivar), [IncidentNews 8741](https://incidentnews.noaa.gov/incident/8741) |
| **Mega Borg 1990** | 12 Navy skimmers + 6,000 ft boom staged at Galveston, 11,300 gal dispersant in 5 sorties, 13,023 bbl recovered, up to ~50 % evaporated or burned | not analysed | [IncidentNews 6748](https://incidentnews.noaa.gov/incident/6748) |
| **Burmah Agate 1979** | fire burned ~48 % of non-lightered oil; 38 % of cargo lightered; skimmers ~1.7 %; booms poor in winter seas | not analysed | [IncidentNews 6253](https://incidentnews.noaa.gov/incident/6253) |
| **Ixtoc I 1979-80** | Mexico: 493 aerial dispersant missions over 1,100 sq mi; Texas: boom + skimmers at inlets, hand cleaning of 162 mi of beach; storm cleared most | not analysed | [IncidentNews 6250](https://incidentnews.noaa.gov/incident/6250) |
| **Eugene Island 2009** | dispersant sorties (504 gal on 27 Jul), ~1,000 gal recovered | pre-approval style small sorties | [IncidentNews 8061](https://incidentnews.noaa.gov/incident/8061) |
| **Main Pass 2023** | 3 skimmers on a > 40 mi offshore slick, no dispersant/burn, no shoreline impact | OpenOil / ours (ML off) match the SW drift and strand nothing; ours + ML strands 62 % (wrong) | [IncidentNews 10692](https://incidentnews.noaa.gov/incident/10692) |

Pattern: on-water tactics removed small fractions of the oil (DWH 16 % combined burn + skim + chemical, Texas City Y 18 %),
booms mostly failed in currents and wind, and most of the effort ended up on shoreline protection and cleanup. Forecasts were
used to *prioritise and stage*, not to promise where oil would be.
