## 2. Core physics

Lagrangian oil models represent the slick as many particles. Each step, a particle is moved by environmental velocities plus random diffusion, and its properties change (weathering, beaching).

**Advection.** Horizontal velocity is typically

`U = U_current(x, z, t) + U_wind_drift + U_Stokes + U_diffusion`

([MEDSLIK-II theory](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/6/1851/2013/gmd-6-1851-2013.pdf); [OpenDrift tutorial](https://opendrift.github.io/tutorial.html)). If the ocean model already resolves the upper-ocean Ekman layer well, an extra wind-drift term can *hurt* skill ([De Dominicis et al. 2013](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/6/1851/2013/gmd-6-1851-2013.pdf), citing Liu et al. 2011 and Huntley et al. 2011).

**Wind drift factor (~3%).** OpenDrift’s maintainer: experiments find surface oil drift of about **3–3.5% of 10 m wind**, *including* Stokes drift of about **1.5% of wind**. So with Stokes on, set `wind_drift_factor ≈ 0.02` (OpenOil default); if Stokes is off or missing, raise it to about **0.03** ([OpenDrift issue #362](https://github.com/OpenDrift/opendrift/issues/362); [parameterised Stokes example](https://opendrift.github.io/gallery/example_parameterized_stokesdrift.html); [wind-drift-from-trajectory example](https://opendrift.github.io/gallery/example_wind_drift_coefficient_from_trajectory.html)). GNOME’s view is the same 3% theoretical value for fresh oil, implemented as a **1–4%** range so the slick spreads downwind ([PyGNOME tech notes](https://gnome.orr.noaa.gov/doc/pygnome/tech_notes.html); [spill windage](https://gnome.orr.noaa.gov/doc/pygnome/scripting/spills.html)). The factor is really a compensation for unresolved shear in the top centimeters, not “sail area”; in OpenDrift it applies at `z = 0` and decays to zero by a configurable depth (default 10 cm) ([issue #362](https://github.com/OpenDrift/opendrift/issues/362)).

**Stokes drift.** Net wave-induced transport. Most ocean models are not wave-coupled, so it must be added from a wave model or a wind/fetch parameterization ([De Dominicis et al. 2013](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/6/1851/2013/gmd-6-1851-2013.pdf); [OpenDrift parameterized Stokes](https://opendrift.github.io/gallery/example_parameterized_stokesdrift.html)). OpenDrift can take Stokes from a wave model or enable `drift:use_tabularised_stokes_drift`.

**Diffusion.** Unresolved turbulence is a random walk. WebGNOME default horizontal eddy diffusivity is **100,000 cm²/s** for a “typical” coastal ocean; lower values keep the slick tighter and raise surface concentrations ([WebGNOME transport](https://gnome.orr.noaa.gov/doc/transport.html)).

**Weathering.** Evaporation, emulsification (water uptake → higher viscosity/density), natural dispersion / wave entrainment into the column, dissolution, biodegradation, sedimentation. These change density and droplet size, which change *horizontal* drift because only surface oil feels full wind and Stokes ([Röhrs et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.5194/os-14-1581-2018)). OpenOil’s default `weathering_model='noaa'` uses the ADIOS/PyGNOME schemes and can set oil type by name, ADIOS ID, or JSON ([OpenOil API](https://opendrift.github.io/autoapi/opendrift/models/openoil/openoil/index.html)). For a Texas City Y–like IFO-380 spill, use an ADIOS IFO 380 record (OpenOil lists types such as `IFO-380LS 2014` and `GENERIC INTERMEDIATE FUEL OIL 300`).

**Beaching.** Particles that hit the shoreline become stranded; they may wash back with some probability, depending on coast type ([De Dominicis et al. 2013](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/6/1851/2013/gmd-6-1851-2013.pdf)). OpenDrift and GNOME both need a land mask / shoreline. For the responder brief, pair beaching locations with **NOAA ESI** shoreline ranks.

**Vertical mixing matters.** Röhrs et al. (2018) show that wave entrainment, turbulence, and resurfacing control how much oil stays in the fast surface layer versus traveling with slower subsurface currents, and that this differs by oil type and weather. That is why a 3-D OpenOil run (even a shallow one) is better than 2-D surface-only drift for a 24–72 h forecast.

## 5. Standard skill metrics

Use these for the T4 backtest. Implement with OpenDrift’s TrajAn helper where possible.

**Liu and Weisberg (2011) skill score** — designed during Deepwater Horizon and now the default in TrajAn (`method='liu-weissberg'`).

- At each time *i*, *dᵢ* is the separation between modeled and observed positions; *lᵢ* is the observed along-track distance from the start (or the segment length used in the cumulative sum).
- Normalized cumulative separation: **s = Σ dᵢ / Σ lᵢ**. Smaller *s* is better; *s* = 0 is a perfect track.
- Skill score: **ss = 1 − s/n** if *s* ≤ *n*, else **ss = 0**. *n* is a tolerance threshold. The usual choice is **n = 1**, i.e. a forecast scores zero once cumulative separation exceeds the cumulative observed path length. Then **ss ∈ [0, 1]**, with 1 perfect.
- Why not raw separation alone? In the 2010 eastern Gulf, Global HYCOM looked worse in the fast Loop Current than on the slower West Florida Shelf if you only plotted kilometers of error; the normalized score correctly flipped that ranking. After 3 days they report mean *s* of **0.74** (deep) and **0.89** (shelf), with *ss* often **0.5–0.9** in the Loop Current / Gulf Stream and **ss < 0.2** on much of the West Florida Shelf.

Sources: [Liu & Weisberg 2011, *JGR Oceans*](https://doi.org/10.1029/2010JC006837), [USF summary](https://ocl.marine.usf.edu/Liu/skill.html), [TrajAn `traj.skill`](https://opendrift.github.io/trajan/generated/xarray.Dataset.traj.skill.html). (The score is not symmetric: always pass the *observed* track as `expected`.)

**Also report, in plain units the judges can read:**

- **Separation distance** (km) at **24, 48, and 72 h** — mean and median, with and without the ML correction. This is what Shen et al. (2022) quoted.
- **Time-to-shore / beaching location error** on any spill or particle that reaches the Texas ESI shoreline.
- Optional: a simple overlap / hit-rate against a Texas City Y overflight or NOAA trajectory polygon from ERMA, if we can download one in time. Treat that as qualitative if the observational slick is sparse.

Do the quantitative score primarily on **GDP tracks** (many independent trajectories). Use **Texas City Y** as the story-driven hindcast (IFO-380, right in the domain). Use **Deepwater Horizon** only as a secondary, clearly labeled deep-blowout comparison.

## 6. Recommendation

**Approach (keep it tight: one region, 24/48/72 h, one user — the on-scene coordinator).**

1. **Physics:** OpenOil with NOAA weathering, Stokes on (parameterized or from GFS Wave / Open-Meteo waves), `wind_drift_factor ≈ 0.02` (raise to ~0.03 only if Stokes is off). Oil type: an ADIOS IFO-380 record for the Galveston scenario. Output particle tracks + oil budget (surface / dispersed / evaporated / stranded).
2. **Forcing:** Hindcasts on **HYCOM GOMb0.04** + GFS or ERA5 winds. Live demo on **RTOFS or ESPC-D-V02** + **GFS**. Open-Meteo is the no-signup fallback, not the skill claim.
3. **ML (the “train a pre-existing model” piece):** Train a **drift correction** on northwestern Gulf **GDP** tracks: either a learned wind-drift factor (OpenDrift already demonstrates this) or a small residual model on OpenOil–observed displacements. Report Liu–Weisberg *ss* and 24/48/72 h separation **with vs. without** the correction. Do not train a SAR detector or a full neural surrogate.
4. **Backtest story:** Skill numbers from GDP; narrative hindcast of **Texas City Y (22 Mar 2014, ~168,000 gal IFO-380, 29.36°N, 94.80°W)**. Overlay ESI-ranked shoreline threat and a short LLM brief (where the oil goes, which shorelines and when, where to stage booms/skimmers).
5. **Context layers:** 2013 Texas Upper Coast ESI; MarineCadastre historical AIS for tanker routes; optional ERMA / MPSR or published slick contour for the demo, not as a trained model.

**Biggest risks**

- **Coastal / bay currents.** HYCOM at ~4 km and Open-Meteo at ~8 km will not resolve Galveston Bay tides, ship channels, or the Texas City dike. Liu & Weisberg already saw much lower trajectory skill on the shelf than in the deep Gulf. Be honest in the brief: skill is for the inner shelf / offshore, not a creek-scale boom plan inside the bay.
- **Drifters are not oil.** GDP (especially drogued) tracks miss windage, emulsification, and beaching. A correction that improves drifter skill may not improve an IFO-380 slick. Call that out and, if possible, sanity-check against Texas City Y overflights.
- **Forcing holes.** CMEMS and ERA5 need accounts; HYCOM reanalysis lags live events by months; THREDDS can be slow. Cache a Gulf subset on the tower on day one.
- **Scope.** A 20-hour window does not fit a trained SAR pipeline, a neural surrogate, or a global 3-D globe. The demo wins on a smooth 72 h deck.gl playback, a clear oil budget, ESI shorelines, and measured skill numbers.
- **Heavy fuel fate.** IFO-380 emulsifies and weathers slowly; if weathering or vertical mixing is off, time-to-shore and the brief will be wrong even if the current field is decent ([Röhrs et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.5194/os-14-1581-2018)).
