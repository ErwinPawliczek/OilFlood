## a) RRT 6 (TX, LA) FOSC dispersant pre-authorization (offshore)

**Document:** "RRT-6 FOSC Dispersant Pre-Approval Guidelines and Checklist", Version 4.0, January 24, 2001 (first approved January 10, 1995). This is the version that was available. The 2015 RRT6 Countermeasures Playbook cites it as rrt6.org "Appendix 11 -- Offshore Dispersant Pre-Authorization Plan". rrt6.org returned a Cloudflare error, so the copy used here is the Texas GLO one.
Fetched URL: https://www.glo.texas.gov/sites/default/files/2025-07/rrt6.pdf

> "The pre-approved area includes offshore waters “from the ten-meter isobath or three nautical miles”, whichever is farthest from the shore, to 200 nautical miles offshore (Exclusive Economic Zone boundary)," — Source: RRT-6 FOSC Dispersant Pre-Approval Guidelines and Checklist v4.0, 2001, https://www.glo.texas.gov/sites/default/files/2025-07/rrt6.pdf (p. 6, printed p. 1)

> "beginning from the Texas-Mexico border and extending through the states of Texas and Louisiana to the boundary between federal Regions IV and VI." — Source: same, p. 6 (printed p. 1)

> "draw a circle about the spill source with a 10 nautical mile radius as a worst-case scenario for surface movement. Hash mark any area within the circle that is in waters less than 10 meters deep or 3 nautical miles from shore." — Source: same, p. 11 (printed p. 6), FOSC Dispersant Use Checklist §2

> "Is the dispersant operational area to be in offshore water that is no less than 10 meters deep and at least 3 nautical miles from the nearest shoreline?" — Source: same, p. 11 (printed p. 6)

> "Winds less than or equal to 25 knots, and Visibility greater than or equal to 3 nautical miles, and Ceiling greater than or equal to 1,000 feet?" — Source: same, p. 12 (printed p. 7), aerial application conditions

> "Dispersant spraying operations are conducted during daylight hours only." — Source: same, p. 6 (printed p. 1)

**Flower Garden Banks (not an exclusion area for dispersants, but with conditions):**

> "Although the Sanctuary and vicinity would not be an oil dispersant exclusion area, if the decision is made to apply oil dispersants in that area, all efforts must be made to apply them in water as deep as possible and as far from the Sanctuary as possible," — Source: same, Appendix C "Dispersant Use Policy of the Flower Gardens National Marine Sanctuary", p. 26

> "excluding the -areas - ... off ~Galveston Texas referred to as "the Flower Gardens" and the "stetson Bank"." — Source: same, Appendix F, NMFS concurrence letter of Sept 8, 1994, p. 41. The text is OCR-garbled. This 1994 NMFS letter excludes FGB and Stetson Bank, while Appendix C of the same document says FGB is not an exclusion area. Treat this as conflicting.

> "The FGBNMS is within the RRT-6 aerial dispersant pre-approval zone, but there has always been a recognition that a dispersant use decision that might impact the" — Source: RRT-6 Guidance for Oil Spill Response Activities within and near the Flower Garden Banks NMS, Appendix 43, March 2019, https://www.glo.texas.gov/sites/default/files/resources/glo/ost/spill-response-resources/rrtvi/appendix43_flower_garden_nms_guidance.pdf (p. 4)

> "The FGBNMS is located approximately 70 to 100 nautical miles offshore of Galveston, Texas," — Source: same, p. 3

> "Current RRT-6 guidance requires a case-by-case approval process for subsea dispersant injection" — Source: same, p. 2

**Nearshore (inside 3 nmi or 10 m): case-by-case approval only, not pre-authorized:**

> "the NSE is defined as the Gulf of Mexico waters seaward of the shoreline but less than 3 miles from shore or less than 10m in depth beginning from the Texas-Mexico border" — Source: RRT-6 Near Shore Environment Dispersant Expedited Approval Process and Checklists, approved January 25, 2005, https://www.glo.texas.gov/sites/default/files/resources/glo/ost/spill-response-resources/rrtvi/rrt6_nearshore_dispersant_eap_signed.pdf (p. 5)

> "This is not a dispersant use pre-authorization, and therefore RRT6 approval is required on a case-by-case basis" — Source: same, p. 5

**Notes/uncertainty:** Version 4.0 (2001) is the newest RRT6 offshore dispersant pre-approval I could fetch, and the 2015 RRT6 Playbook (https://www.glo.texas.gov/sites/default/files/resources/glo/ost/spill-response-resources/additionaldocs/noaa/Region6RRTOilSpillCountermeasuresPlaybook.pdf) still points to it. I could not check whether rrt6.org has a newer version because the site was Cloudflare-blocked. The FGB status is ambiguous: the 2001 Appendix C and the 2019 Appendix 43 say FGB is inside the zone and only asks for "as deep/far as possible", but the 1994 NMFS letter excludes FGB and Stetson Bank. I found no explicit "state waters" exclusion for RRT6; the boundary is the 10 m isobath or 3 nmi, whichever is farther from shore.

## b) RRT 4 (MS, AL, FL) dispersant pre-authorization

**Document:** "Region IV Regional Response Team Ocean and Coastal Waters Dispersant Use Policy", Version 1.0, Letter of Promulgation "DATE of EFFECT: 08 Oct 1996". It sits inside RRT4 Annex J "Oil Spill Countermeasures" on the RRT4 site (uploaded 2018/05).
Fetched URL: https://r4data.response.epa.gov/r4rrt/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/Annex-J-Oil-Spill-Countermeasures.pdf

> "In general pre-authorization exists 3 miles seaward of any land providing that the water depth is at least 10 meters deep. Some special management areas are however, excluded from pre-authorization." — Source: RRT IV Dispersant Use Policy (in Annex J), 1996, URL above (p. 58, Section II-1)

> "The Green zone is defined as any offshore water within federal Region IV in which ALL of the following three conditions apply: 1) the waters are not classified within a "Yellow" or "Red" zone;" — Source: same, p. 58

> "2) the waters are at least three miles seaward of any shoreline, and 3) the waters are at least 10 meters in depth." — Source: same, p. 58

> "Florida state waters extend seaward into the Gulf of Mexico to a distance of nine miles whereas all other state coastal waters in RRT IV, including Florida’s east coast, extend seaward to a distance of three miles." — Source: same, p. 58

> "No case-by-case approval will be required ... for waters greater than 10 meters in depth that extend seaward in exc3ss of three miles on Florida’s west coast" — Source: same, p. 58 ("exc3ss" is the source's own typo)

> "This includes any waters designated as marine reserves, National Marine Sanctuaries, National or State Wildlife Refuges, units of the National Park Service, or proposed or designated Critical Habitats." — Source: same, p. 58 (Yellow zone = case-by-case)

> "c) The waters are less than 10 meters in depth." — Source: same, p. 59 (Yellow zone criterion)

> "Pre-authorization has been given to the OSC for dispersant use decisions in Federal coastal waters." — Source: Region 4 Regional/Area Contingency Plan (Final RCP 2024, posted 05/05/2025), https://r4data.response.epa.gov/r4rrt/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Final_RCP-2024-05052025.pdf (p. 68)

Related (2026 presentation on new rule requirements, no zone values):
> "Must at minimum specify quantities and duration of use; water depth; distance to shoreline, and proximity to populated areas." — Source: EPA/USCG RRT4 Subpart J Dispersants slides, 2026, https://r4data.response.epa.gov/r4rrt/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/EPA_USCG_RRT4_Subpart-J_Dispersants_2026.pdf

**Notes/uncertainty:** The "~2019 or later" RRT4 dispersant policy is NOT VERIFIED. The RRT4 dispersant-use-plan page (https://r4data.response.epa.gov/r4rrt/index.php/dispersant-use-plan) links only the 1996-era policy (1-RRT4DISP.pdf) and older memos. Under the 2023 Subpart J rule, existing pre-authorization plans may be under revision. I did not check what that means for current status.

## c) In-situ burn (ISB) pre-authorization

### RRT 6

> "this area is defined as those parts of the Region 6 coastal zone seaward beginning three miles offshore." — Source: RRT-6 In-Situ Burn Policy for the Offshore Environment, Appendix 13, May 2020, https://www.glo.texas.gov/sites/default/files/resources/glo/ost/spill-response-resources/rrtvi/RRT6_ISB_Policy_Offshore_Appendix13.pdf (p. 4, printed p. 1)

> "these areas are generally offshore and consist of natural banks, hard bottom habitats, artificial reefs, shoals and an area off of Grand Isle, Louisiana." — Source: same, p. 5 (exclusion zones, Appendix F)

> "Until that time, the preauthorization for use of burning agents in conjunction with the ISB technique is covered by the existing RRT-6 guidance document (see RRT-6 Pre-Authorization for In-Situ Burn Part 1)." — Source: same, p. 4 (the 2020 pre-authorization is pending ESA consultation)

> "4. Wave height </= 3 feet  5. Ocean current </= .75 knots" — Source: same, p. 12 (ISB feasibility checklist). The same checklist also lists "1. Oil thickness >/= 2-3 mm" and "3. Wind Speed </= 25 mph".

> "9. Operation poses acceptably low risk to populated areas" — Source: same, p. 12

> "The preapproval allows FOSCs to permit responsible parties to employ the plan seaward of three miles of the coasts of Louisiana and Texas, with areas excluded offshore in the vicinity of certain reefs and an area off Grand Isle, Louisiana." — Source: RRT VI In-Situ Burn Plan Part I (Operations), USCG D8 letter March 14, 1994, https://www.glo.texas.gov/sites/default/files/resources/glo/ost/spill-response-resources/rrtvi/isb_plan_part1_operations.pdf (p. 2)

> "The plan may also be employed inshore of three miles, including bays, lakes, sounds, and rivers, but incident specific RRT approval must be granted in all such cases." — Source: same, p. 2

> "For purposes of applicability, this area is defined as those parts of the Region 6 coastal zone extending out to 3 miles offshore." — Source: RRT-6 In-Situ Burn Policy, Annex 13a (Coastal, non-preauthorized), April 2021, https://www.glo.texas.gov/sites/default/files/resources/glo/ost/spill-response-resources/rrtvi/RRT6_ISB_Policy_Appendix13A.pdf (p. 3)

> "While not specifically excluded, conducting an ISB in a heavily populated area should be pursued cautiously due to the increased potential for exposing people to concentrated levels of particulates." — Source: same, p. 7

> "The FGBNMS is within the in-situ burn exclusion areas - in-situ burning of oil above these sensitive habitats is not permitted by RRT-6 guidance." — Source: RRT-6 FGBNMS guidance, Appendix 43, March 2019 (URL in section a), p. 4

### RRT 4

> "The "A" zone is defined as any area in Region IV, falling exclusively under federal jurisdiction; and not classified as a "B", or "R" zone; which is at least 3 miles seaward from any state coastline; and seaward of any state waters," — Source: RRT IV In-situ Burn Policy (in Annex J), effective 20 Apr 1995, https://r4data.response.epa.gov/r4rrt/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/Annex-J-Oil-Spill-Countermeasures.pdf (p. 262)

> ""B" zones are all areas falling: 1) anywhere within state waters, 2) waters less than 30 feet in depth that contain living reefs, 3) waters designated as a marine reserve, National Marine Sanctuary," — Source: same, p. 262

> "Since Florida state law prohibits pre-authorization of in-situ burning within state waters, an emergency order has been drafted by the state which will allow for rapid case by case approval" — Source: same, p. 263

> "The RRT IV will respond to the OSC's request for authorization to burn in zone "B" within four hours from time of notification." — Source: same, p. 263

**Notes/uncertainty:** For RRT6 ISB, pre-authorization requires at least 3 (nautical) miles offshore plus exclusion zones. Neither RRT6 ISB document gives a numeric water-depth threshold or populated-area distance; "populated areas" is a qualitative checklist item. The 1994 plan says "three miles" on p. 2 and "3 nautical miles" on p. 5. RRT4 ISB pre-authorization covers federal waters only, so off west Florida it starts at 9 nmi. RRT4 has no general depth criterion; 30 ft applies only to living reefs. The RRT4 ISB policy is 1995-era, and I did not verify any newer version.

## d) Boom types, current limits, deflection angles

> "Containment is deploying a boom to contain and concentrate the oil until it can be removed. Deflection is moving oil away from sensitive areas." — Source: NOAA ORR, Characteristics of Response Strategies: A Guide for Spill Response Planning in Marine Environments, Revised 2010 (reprinted March 2013), https://response.restoration.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/Characteristics_Response_Strategies.pdf (p. 39)

> "Diversion is moving oil toward recovery sites that have slower flow, better access, etc. Exclusion is placing boom to prevent oil from reaching sensitive areas." — Source: same, p. 39

> "Booms begin to fail by entrainment when the effective current or towing speed exceeds 0.7 knots perpendicular to the boom. Waves, wind, debris, and ice contribute to boom failure." — Source: same, p. 39

> "Booms with a draft greater than 1/4 the water depth will lose significant amounts of oil from entrainment." — Source: same, p. 22 (shallow water with strong currents)

> "most conventional booms designs are not capable of containing oil against water velocities much in excess of 0.5 ms-1 (1 knot) acting at right angles to it." — Source: ITOPF TIP 03 "Use of Booms in Oil Pollution Response", © 2011, https://www.itopf.org/fileadmin/uploads/itopf/data/Documents/TIPS_TAPS_new/TIP_3_Use_of_Booms_in_Oil_Pollution_Response.pdf (p. 3)

> "In practice, the escape velocity for most booms is around 0.35 ms-1 (0.7 knots) irrespective of skirt depth." — Source: same, p. 3

> "it is feasible to deflect floating oil even in a 1.5 ms-1 current (3 knots) where a boom positioned at right angles to the flow would fail to contain any oil." — Source: same, p. 8

ITOPF TIP 03, Table 2 (p. 9), values transcribed verbatim:

| Current (knots) | Current (m/s) | Max. Angle (degrees) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.7 | 0.35 | 90 |
| 1.0 | 0.5 | 45 |
| 1.5 | 0.75 | 28 |
| 2.0 | 1.0 | 20 |
| 2.5 | 1.25 | 16 |
| 3.0 | 1.5 | 13 |

> "Table 2: Maximum deployment angles to flow direction at different current strengths for bottom tension booms to prevent escape of oil Calculations are based on an escape velocity of 0.7 knots (0.35 m/s) at 90°." — Source: same, p. 9

> "those currents above 0.75 knots moving perpendicular to boom corralling a discharge are strong enough to cause entrainment. ... At currents above 1 knot, boom will lose oil at a very high rate." — Source: RRT-6 ISB Policy Offshore, Appendix 13, May 2020 (URL in section c), p. 23

Boom size by operating environment (from regulation):
> "Boom height—in. 6-18 18-42 18-42 ≥42 (draft plus freeboard)" — Source: 33 CFR Part 155 Appendix B, Table 1 (columns: Rivers & Canals, Inland, Great Lakes, Ocean; significant wave height ≤1, ≤3, ≤4, ≤6 ft), eCFR API https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-11-19/title-33.xml?part=155&appendix=Appendix%20B%20to%20Part%20155 (human page: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-33/chapter-I/subchapter-O/part-155/appendix-Appendix%20B%20to%20Part%20155)

**Notes/uncertainty:** The table values are an exact transcription. The angle follows sin(θ) = 0.7/U, which reproduces all six values. I did not fetch a separate NOAA "Oil Spill Response Booms" web page; the NOAA 2010 guide above covers the boom-type definitions.

> "Containment boom in a quantity equal to twice the length of the largest vessel involved in the transfer and capable of being deployed at the site of oil transfer operations— (A) Within 1 hour of detection of a spill," — Source: 33 CFR 155.1050 (AMPD, lightering/transfer), same URL; continues "when the transfer is conducted between 0 and 12 miles from the nearest shoreline"
